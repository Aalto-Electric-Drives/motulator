class motulator.common.model.ACPort[source]#

Relation between the terminal current and voltage of a load.

The current derivative of the load at the converter terminals is di/dt = Y @ (u - e) in stationary coordinates, where u is the converter voltage. The matrix Y acts on the real and imaginary parts of the space vectors. The converter uses the port to compute its voltage when its gates are blocked.

Parameters:

  • i (complex) – Current (A).

  • e (complex) – Voltage (V) at which the current derivative is zero.

  • Y (ndarray, shape (2, 2)) – Inverse differential inductance matrix (1/H).

class motulator.common.model.CarrierComparison(t_d=0.0, delay=None, single_update=False, N=2**12)[source]#

Bases: TwoLevelModulator[collections.abc.Sequence[float]]

Carrier comparison of a voltage-source converter.

This computes the gate signals and their durations based on the duty ratios. Instead of searching for zero crossings, the switching instants are explicitly computed in the beginning of each half carrier period, allowing faster simulations. The dead time delays the turn-on of the switches, i.e., each leg is blanked for t_d after its switching instant.

Parameters:

  • t_d (float, optional) – Dead time (s), defaults to 0.

  • delay (int, optional) – Computational delay in half carrier periods, defaults to one sampling period, see Modulator.

  • single_update (bool, optional) – Sample and update the duty ratios once per carrier period, defaults to False, see Modulator.

  • N (int, optional) – Amount of the counter quantization levels, defaults to 2**12.

Examples

>>> from motulator.common.model import CarrierComparison
>>> carrier_cmp = CarrierComparison(delay=0)
>>> # First call gives rising edges
>>> t_steps, gates = carrier_cmp(1e-3, [.4, .2, .8])
>>> # Durations of the switching states
>>> t_steps
array([0.00019995, 0.00040015, 0.00019995, 0.00019995])
>>> # Gate signals of the upper switches
>>> gates[:, 0]
array([[0, 0, 0],
       [0, 0, 1],
       [1, 0, 1],
       [1, 1, 1]])
>>> # Second call gives falling edges
>>> t_steps, gates = carrier_cmp(1e-3, [.4, .2, .8])
>>> t_steps
array([0.00019995, 0.00019995, 0.00040015, 0.00019995])
>>> gates[:, 0]
array([[1, 1, 1],
       [1, 0, 1],
       [0, 0, 1],
       [0, 0, 0]])
>>> # Sum of the step times equals T_s
>>> float(np.sum(t_steps))
0.001
>>> # Dead time blanks each leg after its switching instant
>>> carrier_cmp = CarrierComparison(t_d=1e-4, delay=0)
>>> t_steps, gates = carrier_cmp(1e-3, [.5, .5, .5])
>>> np.round(t_steps / 1e-3, 3)  # In ms
array([0.5, 0.1, 0.4])
>>> gates[:, 0]  # Gate signals
array([[0, 0, 0],
       [0, 0, 0],
       [1, 1, 1]])
>>> gates[:, 1]  # Blanking signals
array([[0, 0, 0],
       [1, 1, 1],
       [0, 0, 0]])
schedule(T_half, command)[source]#

Compute the gate signals and their durations.

Parameters:

  • T_half (float) – Half carrier period (s).

  • command (Sequence[float], shape (n_legs,)) – Duty ratios in the range [0, 1].

Returns:

  • t_steps (SwitchingTimes, shape (N,)) – Durations of the intervals (s).

  • gates (GateStates, shape (N, 2, n_legs)) – Gate signals of the upper switches and blanking signals in each interval.

Return type:

tuple[SwitchingTimes, GateStates]

Notes

Simultaneous switching instants are merged, so the number of intervals varies. Blanking continues across half carrier periods, including changes in their length. A reversed command cancels a pending turn-on and starts a new blanking interval. On the first call, the initial commanded state is assumed to already conduct.

class motulator.common.model.CompositeModel(models)[source]#

Bases: motulator.common.model._base.Model[None]

Composite of models sharing subsystems.

The models are coupled through shared subsystem objects, e.g., a DC bus given to the models of a grid converter and a drive. Each model keeps its converter, whose modulator includes the PWM model and the computational delay, and the model of a controlled converter is driven by its own control system in the simulation, see Simulation. A shared subsystem is evaluated once, whatever its names in the models (the composite names the subsystems model.subsystem after their first occurrence), in the order derived from the direct dependencies, see Model.

Parameters:

models (dict[str, Model]) – Models by name. The names are used for the control systems and the results, so they must be valid attribute names (not starting with an underscore, and not t).

class motulator.common.model.ControlledConverter[source]#

Bases: Protocol

Protocol defining the gate control of converters.

A controlled converter is a converter (see Converter) whose switches are driven by a control system. Its modulator converts the command of the control system (e.g., the duty ratios) into the gate states and their durations within the sampling period, see Modulator. The simulation sets the gate states at the switching instants, from which the converter resolves its switching state (e.g., from the gate signals and the phase currents during the blanking). The format of the command and the gate states is up to the converter and its modulator. The control system can block the gates through set_enable at its sampling instants, after which only the diodes conduct (see Converter.blocked). By default, blocking the gates is not supported.

class motulator.common.model.Converter[source]#

Bases: Subsystem, Protocol

Protocol defining the interface for converters.

In addition to the subsystem interface, a converter provides its internal DC current, which a DC bus draws (see DCBus), and the states of its diodes. While only the diodes conduct (blocked is True), the converter voltage out.u_c_ab is determined by the AC port of the load ac_load and the states of the diodes, see ACPort. A current-fed converter (e.g., a diode bridge with a DC choke) also solves its DC voltage from the DC port of its DC load dc_load, see DCPort, and the model evaluates the DC load after the converter. The simulation locates the transitions of the diode states as events, using the margin of the states, and updates the states at the transitions. Since the voltage varies within the integration interval, the model evaluates the signals given by sample (the voltage by default) at the solution points and passes them to extend_history, which also saves the other signals of the converter that are not states.

The diodes and the DC bus are optional capabilities: by default, the gates are never blocked, so there are no diode-state transitions, and the converter cannot be fed from a DC bus. A converter subclassing this protocol implements only the capabilities it has.

class motulator.common.model.DCBus(C_dc, u_dc)[source]#

Bases: motulator.common.model._base.Subsystem

Capacitive DC bus feeding converters.

The DC bus feeds the converters of the models it is given to, see Model. The model connects its converter to the DC bus through connect, which registers the converter in converters and gives the connections of its DC terminals, i.e., the DC-bus voltage to the input u_dc of the converter. The DC bus draws the DC currents of the registered converters, computed from their switching states and AC currents. The DC bus can be shared by the converters of several models, see CompositeModel. An external current, e.g., from a battery or a braking chopper, can also be fed to the DC bus.

Parameters:

  • C_dc (float) – DC-bus capacitance (F).

  • u_dc (float) – Initial DC-bus voltage (V).

class motulator.common.model.DCPort[source]#

Relation between the DC current and the terminal voltage of a current-fed load.

The terminal voltage of the load is u = e + R*i + L*di/dt, where i is the current fed by the converter. A current-fed converter (e.g., a diode bridge with a DC choke) uses the port to solve its DC voltage, see dc_voltage.

Parameters:

  • e (float) – Voltage (V) at zero current and zero current derivative, e.g., the EMF of the load.

  • R (float) – Series resistance (Ω).

  • L (float) – Series inductance (H), e.g., the DC choke.

class motulator.common.model.DiodeBridge(u_dc=None, u_th=0.0)[source]#

Bases: motulator.common.model._base.Converter

Three-phase six-pulse diode bridge.

The diodes conduct according to the currents of the load ac_load: a leg carrying current is clamped to a rail, and a leg without current floats at the voltage keeping its current at zero. The voltage is computed from the AC port of the load, see ACPort. The diode states are updated at their transitions, which the simulation locates as events, see Simulation. Hence, the commutation of the current between the legs follows from the inductance of the load, e.g., an AC filter and the grid. The threshold voltage of the diodes is included in the diode drops. The DC-bus voltage is the input u_dc, which is constant unless a DC-bus model is connected to it (see DCBus and Model). Alternatively, the bridge feeds a DC load dc_load (e.g., an EMF through a DC choke), whose DC port gives the relation between the DC current and the voltage, see DCPort. The bridge then solves its DC voltage together with the leg voltages, and the DC current is its output i_dc. The devices of the legs can be gated through gated, which models thyristors: a floating leg is clamped to a rail only by a gated device.

Parameters:

  • u_dc (float, optional) – Constant DC-bus voltage (V). Defaults to None, in which case the bridge must be fed from a DC-bus model or feed a DC load.

  • u_th (float, optional) – Threshold voltage (V) of the diodes, defaults to 0.

class motulator.common.model.Model(converter, subsystems, connections, dc_bus=None)[source]#

Base class for continuous-time system models.

A model consists of named subsystems and connections between them. The outputs are computed in an order that respects the direct dependencies of the subsystems (see Subsystem.feedthrough) and passed to the connected inputs immediately, so that the outputs are consistent with the inputs of the same evaluation. The order follows the order of the subsystems where the dependencies allow, and a loop of direct dependencies is rejected. The voltage of a blocked converter depends on the port of its load, which depends on the inputs of the load, so the converter is evaluated after the sources of these inputs. The names of the subsystems name their time series in the results, see ModelTimeSeries.

A model can have a converter, either a passive one (e.g., DiodeBridge) or a controlled one (see ControlledConverter), which is driven by a control system, see Simulation. The modulator of the controlled converter includes the computational delay and the PWM model, see Modulator. The control system can also block the gates of the converter, see ControlSystem.set_enable.

Parameters:

  • converter (Converter | None) – Converter, or None if the model has no converter.

  • subsystems (dict[str, Subsystem]) – All subsystems by name, including the converter. The names must be valid attribute names (not starting with an underscore, and not t).

  • connections (dict[tuple[str, str], tuple[str, str]]) – Connections as {(target, input_name): (source, output_name)}, where target and source are the names of the subsystems.

  • dc_bus (DCBus, optional) – DC bus feeding the converter. The DC bus is added to the subsystems under the name dc_bus, and the converter is connected to it, see DCBus.connect. The DC bus can be shared with other models, see CompositeModel. Defaults to None, in which case the converter has a constant DC-bus voltage.

class motulator.common.model.Modulator(delay=None, single_update=False)[source]#

Base class for modulators.

The sampling period consists of one half carrier period with double update and of two with single update. The command passes through the computational delay once per half carrier period, and the gate states of each half carrier period are computed by schedule, which the subclasses implement. The type parameter Cmd is the type of the command, e.g., the duty ratios of a voltage-source converter.

Parameters:

  • delay (int, optional) – Computational delay in half carrier periods from the sampling instant to the loading of the command. Defaults to None, i.e., one sampling period, which the control systems of this package compensate for: 1 with double update and 2 with single update. With single update, delay=1 loads the command at the carrier extremum between the sampling instants.

  • single_update (bool, optional) – Sample and update the command once per carrier period, i.e., the sampling period of the control system equals the carrier period, defaults to False (double update, the sampling period is half the carrier period).

class motulator.common.model.Sensor[source]#

Sensor with a gain error, an offset, and additive Gaussian noise.

The measured signal is y = gain*x + offset + std*n, where x is the actual signal and n is zero-mean white Gaussian noise with unit variance, drawn independently for each channel at each sample. The parameters are scalars or arrays with one element per channel (e.g., per phase), in the units of the measured signal. The noise represents the sampled signal, i.e., std is the standard deviation of the samples after the anti-aliasing filter and the A/D conversion.

Parameters:

  • gain (array_like, optional) – Gain, defaults to 1.

  • offset (array_like, optional) – Offset, defaults to 0.

  • std (array_like, optional) – Standard deviation of the noise, defaults to 0.

  • rng (numpy.random.Generator, optional) – Random number generator, defaults to an unseeded generator. For reproducible results, pass the same seeded generator to all sensors. Sensors with separate generators of the same seed would produce identical noise.

Examples

>>> from motulator.common.model import Sensor
>>> sensor = Sensor(gain=[1.02, 1, 1], offset=[0, 0.1, 0])
>>> sensor([1.0, -0.5, -0.5])
array([ 1.02, -0.4 , -0.5 ])
class motulator.common.model.Simulation(mdl, ctrl=None, show_progress=True, cfg=None, t_start=None)[source]#

Simulation environment.

Each control system is called at its own sampling instants, which gives the sampling period of its next control cycle. The control systems of a composite model can have different sampling periods, and their sampling instants need not coincide. The switching states of the converters are held constant between the sampling and switching instants of all the converters.

Parameters:

  • mdl (Model) – Continuous-time system model.

  • ctrl (ControlSystem | Mapping[str, ControlSystem], optional) – Discrete-time control system of a model with a controlled converter. For a composite model, the control systems of the models with controlled converters by the model names. Defaults to None, i.e., no control systems.

  • show_progress (bool, optional) – Show progress during simulation, defaults to True.

  • cfg (SolverCfg, optional) – Solver configuration parameters.

  • t_start (float | Mapping[str, float], optional) – First sampling instant (s) of the control system or, for a composite model, of the control systems by the model names. The time ctrl.t of the control system is set to this value. Defaults to the initial time of the model, as do the control systems not included in the mapping, in which case ctrl.t is not changed.

class motulator.common.model.Subsystem[source]#

Bases: Protocol

Protocol defining the interface for all subsystems.

A subsystem has the inputs inp, the outputs out, and the states state, typically dataclasses, whose attributes the model reads by name: the connections pass the outputs to the inputs (see Model), and the states are gathered into the state vector of the solver in their declaration order. The class provides methods for setting states, outputs, and computing state derivatives. It also provides methods for extending the state history and creating the time-series representation of the subsystem, which the model names in the results (see ModelTimeSeries). A subsystem whose outputs depend directly on some of its inputs lists these inputs in feedthrough, from which the model derives the evaluation order of the outputs. The state history is a dict of lists, keyed by the names of the state fields. The model initializes it by init_state_history and extends it by extend_state_history. A subclass can keep its other signals in the history as well, under other keys, as a converter does (see Converter).

class motulator.common.model.VoltageSourceConverter(u_dc=None, modulator=None, u_th=0.0, sign=np.sign, u_dc_sensor=None)[source]#

Bases: DiodeBridge, motulator.common.model._base.ControlledConverter

Three-phase voltage-source converter.

The modulator converts the duty ratios of the control system into the gate states of the legs, i.e., the gate signals of the upper switches and the blanking signals, including the computational delay and the dead time, see ZOH and CarrierComparison. The switches are ideal, except for the dead time and the threshold voltage. When a leg is blanked, i.e., neither of its switches conducts, the current direction determines which diode conducts. The leg state is then (1 - sign(i))/2, where i is the phase current in the beginning of the blanking interval. The threshold voltage of the conducting device, assumed equal for the switches and the diodes, reduces each phase voltage by u_th*sign(i), where the current direction is also determined at the latest switching instant. A current within the tolerance of the diode-state transitions has no direction, see current_direction. The DC-bus voltage is the input u_dc, which is constant unless a DC-bus model is connected to it (see DCBus and Model).

When the gates are blocked (inp.enabled is False), all the switches are open, and the converter operates as a diode bridge, see DiodeBridge. The diode states are latched from the current directions when the gates are blocked.

Parameters:

  • u_dc (float, optional) – Constant DC-bus voltage (V). Defaults to None, in which case the converter must be fed from a DC-bus model.

  • modulator (TwoLevelModulator, optional) – Modulator converting the command of the control system into the gate states, see TwoLevelModulator. Defaults to ZOH(), i.e., the switching-cycle averaged model of the duty ratios without the dead time and with the computational delay of one sampling period. A modulator keeps its state, so each converter needs its own.

  • u_th (float, optional) – Threshold voltage (V) of the power devices, defaults to 0.

  • sign (Callable[[np.ndarray], np.ndarray], optional) – Function of the phase currents (A) determining the leg states during blanking, defaults to np.sign. A smooth function, such as 2/pi*arctan(i/i_d), can be used to model the effect of parasitic capacitances, for example.

  • u_dc_sensor (Sensor, optional) – DC-bus voltage sensor, defaults to an ideal measurement.

property blocked: bool#

class motulator.common.model.ZOH(t_d=0.0, delay=None, single_update=False)[source]#

Bases: TwoLevelModulator[collections.abc.Sequence[float]]

Zero-order hold of the duty ratios of a voltage-source converter.

The dead time reduces the gate signal duty ratios of both switches of a leg by t_d/(2*T_half), leaving the leg blanked for the fraction t_d/T_half of the half carrier period T_half, unless the leg does not switch. Short pulses are suppressed, and the blanking fraction is computed from the remaining on-times of both switches. This is a switching-cycle averaged model, not a model of switching transients.

Parameters:

  • t_d (float, optional) – Dead time (s), defaults to 0.

  • delay (int, optional) – Computational delay in half carrier periods, defaults to one sampling period, see Modulator.

  • single_update (bool, optional) – Sample and update the duty ratios once per carrier period, defaults to False, see Modulator.

motulator.common.model.dc_voltage(port, dc, diodes)[source]#

Solve the voltage between the rails of a current-fed bridge.

The DC current of the bridge is the current of the legs clamped to the positive rail (equally, the negative of the current of the legs clamped to the negative rail). Its derivative follows from the AC port and the leg voltages, which depend on the rail voltage, see leg_voltages. The rail voltage satisfying the DC port u_dc = e + R*i_dc + L*di_dc/dt is solved from this linear relation.

Parameters:

  • port (ACPort) – Relation between the terminal current and voltage of the AC-side load.

  • dc (DCPort) – Relation between the DC current and the voltage of the DC-side load.

  • diodes (ndarray, shape (3,)) – Diode states of the legs, see leg_voltages.

Returns:

Voltage (V) between the rails.

Return type:

float

motulator.common.model.update_diodes(port, dc, diodes=None, gated=None)[source]#

Update the diode states at a transition or latch them when the gates are blocked.

A conducting leg whose current has reversed starts to float, and a floating leg whose voltage is outside the rails is clamped to the rail by its diode. When the gates are blocked, the legs carrying current conduct in the direction of the current, and the other legs float. A device with a gate signal (e.g., a thyristor) clamps the leg only if it is gated, and it conducts until its current reverses.

Parameters:

  • port (ACPort) – Relation between the terminal current and voltage of the load.

  • dc (float | DCPort) – Voltage (V) between the rails, or the DC port of a current-fed bridge, from which the voltage is solved, see dc_voltage.

  • diodes (ndarray, shape (3,), optional) – Diode states of the legs, see leg_voltages. Defaults to None, which latches the states from the directions of the currents.

  • gated (ndarray, shape (2, 3), optional) – Gate signals of the upper and lower devices of the legs. Defaults to None, i.e., all the devices are gated (diodes).

Returns:

Updated diode states.

Return type:

ndarray, shape (3,)
